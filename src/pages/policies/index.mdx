## Code of conduct

SLOP4000 studies hostile software as an engineering discipline, and that
subject matter is exactly why its code of conduct is not boilerplate.
Every lab, every lecture example and both assessments are scoped to
deliberately benign samples run inside simulated, disposable environments:
curiosity about how the material works is the whole point of enrolling,
but real malice, a real target, or code built to do real harm is never in
scope, on this course or any other. Work that reaches outside a simulated
environment — against a real host, a real network, or another student's
system — is treated as a conduct matter first and a grading one second,
regardless of how competent the work is.

The same standard extends to how you discuss the material outside class:
techniques covered here are taught for analysis and defence, and sharing
anything built on this course's material for use against a real target is
a breach of the same conduct this section sets, not a separate rule.

## AI use and integrity

A course whose subject is deception has an unusually direct stake in
knowing whose work is whose. You may use generative tools to explore
ideas, check your understanding, or tidy a draft, but the architecture
decisions in Assessment 1 and the infiltration, persistence and evasion
choices you defend in Assessment 2 have to be judgements you made and can
explain, not output you are relaying. Where a tool materially shaped a
submission, say so plainly in the write-up rather than leaving it to be
inferred — undisclosed use that stands in for your own analysis is treated
as a breach of academic integrity, the same as it would be on any other
piece of assessed work.

Assessment 2 in particular is judged on the report as much as the outcome:
a flag retrieved without a defensible account of how you found and kept
access to it does not demonstrate what the assessment is designed to
measure.

## Communication

There is no discussion board standing between you and the people running
this course. General questions about the syllabus, the labs or either
assessment go directly to Professor Sidorov, the Course Convenor, or
Professor Al-Fulani, the Co-Convenor — both are named on the People page,
and either can answer. Because each week's lab builds directly on the
lifecycle stage the last one covered — a threat model feeding an
architecture, an architecture feeding an execution strategy, and so on
through to the operational-security work in the closing weeks — a question
left unresolved for a week compounds rather than waiting quietly, so raise
it as soon as it comes up rather than at the following week's lab.

## Enrolment

SLOP4000 runs weekly from 27 July to 12 November 2027, with a
mid-semester break built into the schedule rather than a fixed weekly
cadence throughout. Because the ten labs are a single progression — each
one assumes the lifecycle stage the previous lab produced, from an initial
threat model through to the attribution and operational-security work
that closes it — enrolling after Week 1 means arriving without the
foundation the next lab is written to assume, not simply missing a mark.
Anyone enrolling late should contact Professor Sidorov or Professor
Al-Fulani before their first lab so the gap can be named honestly rather
than discovered partway through it.
