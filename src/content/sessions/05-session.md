Getting in is a distinct engineering problem from what happens afterwards,
and it is usually the operator's highest-risk moment. This session surveys
how that first entry actually happens.

## Before the session

Nothing new to bring — Lab 4 supplies its own prepared evidence for each
scenario.

## In the session

The lecture covers the vector families: social engineering, technical
exploitation, physical media, network exposure and supply-chain compromise,
plus the case-study method for reading which one was used after the fact.
Lab 4 then gives you prepared evidence for five simulated scenarios —
phishing, a vulnerability, removable media, a network attack, a
supply-chain compromise — and asks you to identify and compare the vector
each one relies on.

## Afterwards

You should be able to say which vector left the most artefacts and which
needed the least interaction from the target. Persistence — staying in once
you're in — is next.
